About the ritual

Sanni Yakuma is a traditional healing ritual of southern and western Sri Lanka, performed from dusk to dawn. It opens with the Ata Paliya, eight dances that bless the patient. Then, in the Daha Ata Sanniya, masked dancers portray the eighteen Sanni demons, each believed to cause a particular illness. The demons appear one by one, first frightening, then humiliated as comic figures, and are persuaded to leave the patient. Maha Kola Sanniya, the chief disease-demon, comes last; old masks show him surrounded by his eighteen attendant demons. The ritual is still performed today, mostly along the south coast, though more often as a cultural performance than as an exorcism.

This page borrows the ritual's shape, not its sacred purpose. The humour is aimed at the code sicknesses, as the ritual itself mocks the demons, never at the tradition or the people who keep it. Read more: Sanni Yakuma on Wikipedia, and the Maha Kola Sanniya mask in the Science Museum Group collection.
